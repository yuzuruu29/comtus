## Design Context

### Users
Students and board-exam reviewers (nursing, LET, engineering, CPA) who sit with PDFs, lecture notes, and messy highlights. They use Comtus to ground chat in their own sources, drill quizzes, and polish notes — often late, often for long stretches, often with the tab left open.

### Brand Personality
Warm scholar. Crafted, lamplit, precise. Three words: **warm, scholarly, alive**. The interface should feel like a library at midnight with one lamp still burning — not a hype startup, not a grayscale productivity tool, and not a cartoon game. Copy stays second person, calm, no exclamation marks.

### Aesthetic Direction
**The scholar's desk**, following the September 4 frontier study desk specification. Warm paper, clear ink, restrained brass, Literata reading and editorial headings, Instrument Sans interface copy. The composer is the desk's lamp. The landing opens immediately and demonstrates a clearly labelled sample notebook. The desk uses readable panes, a quiet shelf, and native controls. Help and legal pages share the same navigation and typography. Reuse `src/ui` and `src/styles/tokens.css`. Avoid telemetry decoration, forced introductions, excessive cards, glossy gradients, and mono form labels. The optional companion starts clear of the work; users control its placement and size.

### Landing Motion (October 2026)

The landing no longer plays a staged introduction. The headline typesets itself
in (a line rising into its window, then a drawn pen stroke), and everything below
arrives on native scroll timelines: rows rise and fade, ruled margins are drawn,
and a brass ribbon on the navigation bar marks how far the page has been read.
The interactive notebook still opens already written and only moves once a visitor
asks it something. Motion follows the saved budget and the system reduced-motion
setting; every static state is the finished page.

### Landing Motion References (September 8, 2026)

The landing uses an original animated paper composition: gather source sheets, connect a cited idea, then turn a recall card. Oversized editorial type and a drawn underline frame the scene. StudyMotion owns its visual stages; the interactive notebook demo remains separate and labelled.

References inspected on the web: [mymind](https://mymind.com/) for expressive editorial scale, [Readymag](https://readymag.com/) for layered motion composition, and [Animations on the Web](https://animations.dev/) for timing and easing judgment. Borrow composition and motion principles, not branded assets.

Keep manual stage controls, pause and replay. Respect system reduced motion and the saved motion preference. Pause when offscreen or the document is hidden. Static states must remain complete.

The study-tools section is an illustrated field guide: a source sheet, an unboxed mind map, and a keyboard-operable recall card on a sage/paper surface. Native scroll timelines draw the branches and place the paper pieces. Stack the composition through 940px. Verify real scroll progress: a full-height body with overflow-y auto traps view timelines and sticky navigation, so the landing alone uses overflow-x clip and overflow-y visible. Keep SVG paths and HTML annotations aligned to the same map box.

### Design Principles
1. Color carries meaning (tool kind, state, milestone accent) and warmth — never decoration for its own sake.
2. Compad is a sage notebook with ivory pages, a brass bookmark, and an expressive ink face. It stays optional. Users can move and resize it throughout an active notebook. Keep the character and its compact chat within the viewport, preserve chosen placement, and provide a position reset. Its chat shares the notebook's conversation, sources, model, and draft.
3. Achievements are study craft ("the first page on the desk"), never streaks-as-guilt or XP.
4. Motion makes state changes clear. Respect reduced motion and the separate SFX mute. Reading size controls affect reading, while the interface stays stable. Never hide landing content behind a timed introduction.
5. Local-first: milestone progress is derived from the on-device library, stored beside settings.
